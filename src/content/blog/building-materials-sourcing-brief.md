Start a furniture or building-product sourcing project with a line-item specification, intended installation location, quantities, required documents and named approval responsibilities. A photo and a room count are not enough to compare manufacturers. Confirm the technical, import and installation review for each item before purchasing, especially when several suppliers or product categories must fit the same project.

This is an educational brief framework. Sourcing Lab USA's core specialties remain clothing, textiles and packaging. Other categories can be discussed for feasibility; this guide does not establish a completed furniture, construction or hospitality project, a supplier network in those categories, or a guarantee that a proposed item can be supplied.

## How is a project brief different from a product list?

A project brief connects each item to the place it will be used and the requirements it must satisfy there. Give items reference numbers that match drawings, room schedules and sample requests. Identify whether quantities represent pieces, sets, area or another unit. Record who owns the measurements and who approves technical compatibility.

For a hotel or restaurant project, separate guest-room, public-area and back-of-house requirements where relevant. A decorative reference can communicate style while leaving cleaning, durability, installation and replacement requirements unresolved. Ask the project team to identify these before asking suppliers to price the item.

## What information should each category include?

Use this matrix to prepare questions for the supplier and the project's qualified designers, installers or product specialists. It is not a list of compliance determinations or services automatically offered by Sourcing Lab USA.

| Category | Specification to collect | Decision owner to identify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Furniture | Dimensions, intended use, construction, materials, finish and assembly | Buyer and responsible designer or specialist |
| Kitchen cabinets | Drawing revision, module sizes, hardware, panels and appliance interfaces | Designer and installer |
| Bathroom products | Dimensions, connection details, materials and intended installation | Designer, installer and relevant specialist |
| Tiles and stone | Material, dimensions, thickness, finish, intended location and batch requirements | Designer and installer |
| Doors and windows | Opening schedule, configuration, hardware and required performance evidence | Responsible project professional |
| Lighting | Intended location, electrical specification, mounting and required documentation | Electrical designer or qualified specialist |

Confirm the exact products and applicable jurisdiction with those professionals. Do not infer suitability from a product name, export history or an unrelated certificate.

## Which U.S. product questions need early review?

For products containing composite wood, ask whether the item falls within the scope of the EPA's [formaldehyde emission standards for composite wood products](https://www.epa.gov/formaldehyde/formaldehyde-emission-standards-composite-wood-products) and which documentation the responsible parties need. This is a review trigger, not a determination that every cabinet or furniture item has the same requirements.

For covered consumer products, use the [CPSC certification and testing guidance](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Testing-Certification/General-Use-Products-Certification-and-Testing) to direct the product-specific review. Local installation and building requirements may require separate review by the responsible project professionals. A supplier's general statement of “U.S. compliance” should be replaced with the exact requirement, applicable product and evidence.

Import classification and charges also require a product-specific review. Provide materials, construction, use, origin and pricing to the relevant specialist, using the [official U.S. tariff schedule](https://hts.usitc.gov/) as a reference. Do not apply a generic China tariff percentage to the entire project budget.

## How do you compare quotations across several suppliers?

Use one schedule with item code, specification revision, unit, quantity, included hardware or components, sample scope, packing, price basis and open questions. Keep substitutions visible. A cabinet price excluding hardware or a lighting price excluding a specified component is not directly comparable with a complete-item quote.

Then assign ownership of interfaces. Who verifies that a cabinet opening matches the appliance? Who approves the door handing? Who confirms a tile sample's relationship to the production batch? These are original planning questions for the project team, not assurances that a supply partner provides engineering services.

## How should delivery and installation be planned?

Ask for packing details that connect cartons or crates to item references and receiving locations. Confirm who handles consolidation if needed, transport, import arrangements, unloading, storage and installation. These responsibilities and costs must be agreed rather than inferred from a factory quote.

Create a receiving sequence based on the actual project plan. Do not use a production estimate as a promised installation date. Send the item schedule, intended U.S. location and use, drawings or references, quantities, budget basis and target dates.
