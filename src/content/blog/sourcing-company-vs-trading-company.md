A trading company buys from factories and resells to you, with a margin on the goods. A sourcing company finds, assesses and prices factories for you, and then supplies the order under its own contract. Both can deliver the product. What differs is who is accountable for the factory relationship, how transparent the price is, and what happens when the factory fails.

Ask any supplier one question before you compare prices: who owns the factory relationship, and what is the margin on? The answer should be checkable in the quotation.

## How a trading company works

A trading company is a reseller. It holds relationships with factories, buys at a negotiated price and sells to you at a higher one. The strengths are real: one contact, English-speaking sales, consolidated products from several factories, and speed on catalogue items.

The costs are equally real:

- The margin sits inside the unit price, and you cannot see it. You do not know what the factory charges.
- The factory knows you as the trading company's customer, not yours. If the relationship ends, you restart.
- When quality fails, you have a contract with the reseller, not with the manufacturer — and the reseller's liability is limited to what the contract says.

## How a sourcing company works

A sourcing company performs the discovery and vetting you would otherwise do yourself: identifying factories that make the product type, verifying registrations and machinery, comparing prices, managing samples and following production. Sourcing Lab USA then supplies the goods under its own contract — the company named in your quotation, in France or China, contracts with you and invoices the order.

What that changes:

- Factory options come with a stated basis: which factory, what capacity, what the quotation covers.
- Sample economics are transparent: what the sample costs, and what the production price becomes.
- Production follow-up is the supplier's contractual work, not a favour negotiated through an intermediary.

## What to check before signing either

| Question | Trading company | Sourcing company |
|---|---|---|
| Who contracts with me? | The reseller | The company named in the quotation |
| Is the factory identified? | Often not, and not yours | Yes, with its role stated |
| Where is the margin? | Inside the unit price | Stated — the fee is the fee |
| Who follows production? | Whoever the reseller assigns | The contracted supplier |

## The question to ask in both cases

"Give me the factory name and let me see the factory's price — or explain why not." A trading company will usually decline. A sourcing company running a transparent supply will state what is in the quotation, what is added only on request, and what the fee covers.

Transparency is the deliverable that separates the two models — more than any claim on a website.
