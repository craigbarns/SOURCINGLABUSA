When U.S. brands start sourcing custom apparel and textiles from China, two terms immediately dominate the conversation: **Tech Packs** and **MOQ** (Minimum Order Quantity). 

A tech pack records the product specification; MOQ is the minimum quantity quoted for a particular product or variant. Review them together, because materials, colors, construction and setup requirements can affect the quantity a supplier can propose.

## What is a Tech Pack?

A Tech Pack (Technical Packet) is the blueprint for your textile product. It is a comprehensive document that contains every detail a factory needs to manufacture your garment or textile item. 

A standard tech pack includes:
- **Technical Sketches:** Flat drawings of the front, back, and details.
- **Bill of Materials (BOM):** A list of all fabrics, threads, buttons, zippers, and labels required.
- **Measurements and Grading:** Exact dimensions for every size you plan to produce.
- **Construction Details:** Stitching types, seam finishes, and placement of labels.
- **Colorways:** Specific Pantone codes or color references.

## Why the Tech Pack Dictates the MOQ

Many brands ask, *"What is your standard MOQ?"* before providing any product details. The reality is that for custom textiles, the MOQ is rarely a fixed number—it is determined by the details in your tech pack.

Here is why:
1. **Fabric Minimums (MOQ of the Mill):** If your tech pack specifies a custom-dyed fabric or a unique blend, the factory must purchase that fabric from a mill. Ask whether fabric minimums apply to the proposed material and color, and how those minimums affect the garment quantity.
2. **Setup Costs:** Custom hardware (like branded zippers) or complex printing (like all-over sublimation) requires significant setup time. Factories require higher MOQs to absorb these setup costs.

## How to Lower Your MOQ Using Your Tech Pack

If you are a startup or testing a new product line, you likely want to keep your MOQ as low as possible. You can design your tech pack strategically to achieve this:

- **Use Available Market Fabrics:** Instead of custom-dying a fabric to a specific Pantone color, ask your sourcing partner to find an "available market fabric" (in-stock fabric) that closely matches your requirement. This may change the minimum commitment; availability and quantities still need confirmation.
- **Standardize Hardware:** Use standard buttons and zippers rather than custom-molded hardware.
- **Consolidate Colors:** Launch with one or two core colors rather than five different colorways. MOQs are often calculated *per color, per style*.

## The Importance of a Clear Brief

A detailed tech pack helps identify assumptions before an order is approved. When a factory has to guess your requirements, they price in risk, which often results in higher costs and stricter MOQs. By presenting a professional, complete specification, you demonstrate that you are a serious buyer, which can give you more leverage during negotiations.
