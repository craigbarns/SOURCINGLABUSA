The minimum order quantity (MOQ) is the smallest production quantity a supplier will accept for a specific quotation. In custom packaging, ask whether it applies per structure, material, artwork or finished item. Setup, print, finish, tooling and variant splits can change the minimum. Compare the complete order commitment and the stock you can use, not just the unit price.

The most useful starting point is a clear brief rather than a target quantity in isolation.

## Why MOQs vary
Custom packaging may require setup steps before the first production unit is made. Depending on the project, these can include artwork preparation, color matching, printing setup, cutting or forming tools, material purchasing, and hand assembly. A supplier needs a production run that makes those steps viable.

Two boxes that look similar can have very different MOQs if one uses standard dimensions and a simple print while the other needs a new structure, special material, detailed finish, or several assembly steps.

## Questions to include in your RFQ
Ask the supplier to state:

- MOQ for the quoted specification;
- price breaks at practical quantities;
- one-time tooling or setup charges;
- sample cost and timing;
- included and excluded finishing steps;
- carton details, production lead time, and shipping terms.

## Ways to explore a lower commitment
If the first MOQ is too high for the project, consider options that preserve the product objective:

1. Use a standard structure or size where it still protects the product.
2. Compare a simpler finish or printing method with the initial concept.
3. Separate the first sample run from production quantities.
4. Ask for quantity breakpoints and compare total order cost, not only unit price.

## Is MOQ per order, per design or per component?

Ask the supplier to identify the unit behind each minimum. A box, insert and label may have different production requirements. Several artwork versions can share a structure while still requiring separate print setups. A total of 2,000 boxes does not necessarily satisfy a 500-unit minimum for each of five distinct versions.

| Question | Why it affects the commitment |
| --- | --- |
| Does MOQ apply per structure or artwork? | Shared dimensions may not combine separate print runs |
| Can variants share material or tooling? | A common input may change the quoted basis |
| Are inserts and labels included? | Component minimums may exceed the assembled-unit quantity |
| Are samples part of the production quantity? | Do not count them unless the quotation does |
| Can repeat orders use the same setup? | Confirm retained tooling, revision and costs |

## A hypothetical first-order comparison

Assume a buyer is comparing two fictional quotes for the same packaging specification. Option A is 500 units at $2.80 plus $150 setup; Option B is 1,000 units at $2.10 plus the same setup. Neither quote is a Sourcing Lab USA offer or an industry benchmark.

| Option | Goods and setup only | Allocated cost per ordered unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: 500 units | $1,550 | $3.10 |
| B: 1,000 units | $2,250 | $2.25 |

Option B has a lower unit cost but requires $700 more for this scope and 500 additional units. If the buyer can use only 500 units before the artwork changes, the extra stock matters. Both comparisons still exclude transport, import and other order-specific costs; use the [landed-cost worksheet](/blog/calculating-landed-costs-merchandise) before deciding.

## How can you negotiate without weakening the brief?

Separate non-negotiable requirements from optional finishes. Request a written alternative for a standard structure, fewer artwork versions, shared materials or a simpler finish, then compare the revised specification and total commitment. Avoid agreeing to an unspecified substitution solely to meet a target MOQ.

Record each alternative with its quantity, unit price, setup, sample scope, packing and expiry. A small sample run does not automatically prove that the same economics or process applies to production. Review [sample and quality checkpoints](/blog/quality-control-checklist) before ordering.
